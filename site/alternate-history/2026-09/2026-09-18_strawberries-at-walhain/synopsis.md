# Synopsis

Napoleon breaks Wellington's centre on the evening of 18 June 1815 and takes Brussels three days later, but the victory buys months rather than a war. Austrian and Russian armies cross the Rhine that summer, and the campaign in Alsace grinds down a France that has no more men to give. Then a volcano that erupted the previous April cancels the summer of 1816 across the northern hemisphere, and empty granaries do what armies could not. The peace signed in 1817 is Metternich's arithmetic: the Bourbons cannot be put back on bayonets that just won, so the throne goes to a six-year-old with a Habsburg mother, and Napoleon sails for the Delaware. His son rules eleven years under a regency, comes of age, and dies of tuberculosis at twenty-one, leaving the crown to a cousin nobody had planned for. In French the battle is always called Mont-Saint-Jean, and the village a mile north gives its name to nothing.
