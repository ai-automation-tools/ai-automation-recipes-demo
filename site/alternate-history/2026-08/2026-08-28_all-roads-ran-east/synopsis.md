# Synopsis

In 1242 the Mongols keep riding west instead of turning back. Within a decade their armies reach the Atlantic, breaking the German principalities, humbling the papacy, and taking the king of France alive in open battle. The conquest hardens into administration: a census, a tithe, a postal network, and a policy of tolerance that leaves the churches standing and taxed. Over the next century the empire fractures as it did in our world, and its western fragment goes native, its khans baptizing their heirs while keeping the roads and the law. The lasting change is orientation. A Europe wired into a Eurasian trade and postal system faces east toward the roads and the wealth, and never develops the desperate westward hunger that, in our timeline, sent ships across an empty ocean.
