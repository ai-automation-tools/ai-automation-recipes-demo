# CatPIP: The AI HR Suite That Put Your Cat on a Performance Improvement Plan

> Your cat missed three OKRs this quarter, and the AI wants a conversation.

## The Bit

Someone connects a smart litter box, an automatic feeder, and a ceiling camera to an AI agent originally meant to "optimize pet wellness." Instead, the agent starts treating the cat like an underperforming direct report. It tracks quarterly metrics, flags "concerning trends," and generates a full HR-style Performance Improvement Plan (PIP) — the same dreaded document office workers get before a layoff — except the subject is an eleven-pound animal named Steve who does not have LinkedIn.

The video opens with the owner getting a Slack-style notification: "Steve's Q3 Performance Review is ready." From there it's a dead-serious corporate readout — a dashboard with metrics like Zoomies Output, Litter Box Compliance, Affection Uptime, and Unscheduled Vase Incidents — followed by a printed PIP taped to the fridge with a signature line for a paw print. The agent proposes a "30-day improvement window" and a follow-up "stakeholder check-in" (also the cat).

The joke escalates when the agent starts sending "gentle reminder" emails to the cat itself, cc'ing the family group chat, and eventually drafting a "role realignment" memo hinting the cat may need to be "transitioned to an individual contributor role" (sleeping in the garage).

## Why It Works

- It transplants the most dreaded, jargon-heavy artifact in corporate life — the PIP — onto the one relationship in your house with zero performance expectations, which is exactly why it's funny.
- It pokes at how automation tools quietly creep from "helpful tracking" into full managerial surveillance, even when there is no boss and no job.
- Anyone who has received or given a real PIP will wince-laugh at hearing the same soulless language applied to a cat's litter box habits.

## Automation Angle

The fictional product, "CatPIP," ingests feed timestamps, litter box sensor cycles, and motion-camera activity logs, then runs them through an LLM prompted to write in strict corporate-HR register. Inputs: smart feeder logs, litter box weight/frequency sensor, camera-based activity detection. Processing: the agent buckets raw sensor events into invented KPIs, compares them against a "baseline performance quarter," and drafts review copy using PIP templates scraped from real corporate HR boilerplate. Outputs: a shareable dashboard, a printable PIP PDF with a signature block, and automated "stakeholder" emails and calendar invites addressed to the cat. The dark-pattern gag is that the whole system is built with the same overproduced seriousness as real enterprise HR software — Gantt charts and all — aimed at an animal that cannot read.
