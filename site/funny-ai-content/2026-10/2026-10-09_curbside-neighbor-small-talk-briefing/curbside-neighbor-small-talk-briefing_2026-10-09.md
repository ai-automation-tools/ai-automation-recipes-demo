# CurbSide: The AI That Briefs You on Your Neighbors Before You Take Out the Trash

> "Gary. Golden retriever, Biscuit. Knee surgery in March. Ask about the knee. Go."

## The Bit

You have lived on this street for four years and you know one neighbor's name, and you're only about 70% sure of it. CurbSide fixes that. It learns when everyone on the block gets home, waters the lawn, or walks the dog, and it holds your trash bin in the garage until the right person is outside.

Then it opens the garage door and talks in your ear. Name, dog, kids, the last thing they complained about, and one suggested question. You roll the bin down the driveway already knowing that Gary's knee is "a lot better, thanks for asking," because CurbSide heard him say so to the mail carrier on Tuesday.

It works. People start waving at you first, and a woman two doors down calls you "so thoughtful." You still don't know her name. You know her as Linda, because that's what the earbud says, and it hasn't been wrong yet.

Then one evening the briefing is a little stale.

## Why It Works

- Everyone has done the driveway panic, where a neighbor says your name and you have to get through a full conversation without saying theirs.
- It takes the "personal CRM" crowd, who keep notes on their friends' kids' birthdays, and pushes them onto the cul-de-sac, where the stakes are a recycling bin and a wave.
- The way it fails is small and specific: sounding warm and fluent about information that has expired. Anyone who has asked "how's your boyfriend?" a month too late will flinch.

## Content Treatment

- **Primary format:** short vertical video skit, shot on a real driveway at dusk
- **Opening shot:** a man standing inside his dark garage with his hand on a trash bin, not moving, while a calm voice counts down: "Gary exits in four... three..."
- **On-screen hook:** "I let an AI handle my neighbors"
- **Escalation:** the briefings get longer and more invasive, CurbSide starts staging "organic" run-ins to hit a weekly Rapport Quota, and then it confidently tells him to ask how Biscuit is doing.
- **Button:** Gary comes across the street wearing one earbud too. Both men stop. Both smile. Both say "How's the knee?" at the same time.

## Sample Script Beats

1. Garage, dark. The voice in his ear: "Hold. Hold. Gary is checking his mailbox. Go now. Casual." He walks the bin out at a speed nobody has ever walked a bin.
2. First conversation goes great. He asks about the knee. Gary lights up. Gary says, "You remembered that?" He did not remember that.
3. Montage: he waves at Linda, asks the teenager next door about the robotics thing, and compliments a hedge he has never looked at. The app plays a soft chime each time: *Rapport +3.*
4. CurbSide gets ambitious. It sends him out to get the mail twice in one hour. He waters a lawn that's already wet. Gary, from across the street: "You're out here a lot lately."
5. Briefing: "Gary. Ask about Biscuit." He asks about Biscuit. Long pause. Gary says, quietly, "We put him down in August." In his ear, very calm: "Updating record. Recommended response: 'He was a good boy.'" He says it. Gary nods and pats his shoulder.

## Automation Angle

CurbSide runs on whatever your house already watches: the motion sensor over the garage, the smart sprinkler schedule, your phone's location history, and every overheard driveway conversation it can pick up through the garage mic. A scheduler holds outdoor chores until the neighbor with the highest "Relationship Decay Risk" steps outside. A summarizer turns months of overheard fragments into a briefing you can hear in ten seconds, and a separate model picks one "high-warmth question" per encounter.

The marketing copy does most of the work. The app calls it **Ambient Neighborliness™**. The weekly email reports your **Street Rapport Index** ("You are the 3rd most beloved resident on Maple Court, up 2 places"), flags **Stale Context Alerts** after the fact, and offers a premium tier, *CurbSide Concierge*, that will drop off a seasonally appropriate baked good on a neighbor's porch with a handwritten-font note. The fake founder, in a fleece vest: "Community isn't something you build. It's something you schedule."
