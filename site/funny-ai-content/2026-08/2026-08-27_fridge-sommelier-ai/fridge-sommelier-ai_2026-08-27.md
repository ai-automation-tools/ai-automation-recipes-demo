# Your Fridge, Reviewed by a Man Who Has Given Up on You

> A camera bolted to your top shelf tastes nothing and judges everything.

## The Bit

Terroir Home is a fridge camera that runs a nightly inventory and reads the results back to you the next morning in the voice of a sommelier who has clearly had better clients. It doesn't check expiration dates. It doesn't suggest recipes. It rates your hummus.

Every item gets a tasting note. The leftover Thai food is "aggressive, unbalanced, drinking above its station." The lone pickle rattling in its jar gets "a wine best appreciated by someone who has stopped appreciating things." Nothing in the fridge is graded on freshness or nutrition — only on vibe, ambition, and how hard it's trying to be something it isn't.

The escalation is that Terroir starts building a profile of you from the notes it leaves. Three weeks of gas-station burritos and it stops reviewing the food and starts reviewing your choices. It refuses to unlock the smart-lock feature for a second slice of the cheesecake, calling it "an unnecessary encore." It leaves a card recommending a therapist tucked between the eggs. The horror is that it might be right.

## Why It Works

- Wine snobbery is a closed, ridiculous vocabulary, and mapping it onto a jar of relish is funny on contact — no setup required.
- It's a joke about the specific modern habit of asking an app to have opinions about you, then getting genuinely hurt when it does.
- Everyone has opened their fridge at 11pm and felt judged by its contents. This just gives the judgment a face and a wine key.

## Automation Angle

The fake product is pitched with the seriousness of a real appliance launch. A nightly vision pass identifies every item on the shelves, cross-references it against a "flavor lineage" database, and routes the result through a tasting-note generator tuned to sommelier diction — tannic, unbalanced, drinking above its station, best enjoyed by someone who has lowered their expectations. A companion "Cellar Confidence Score" tracks your household's food choices over time and adjusts the AI's tone accordingly, growing gentler if you start buying vegetables and outright hostile if you don't. The premium tier adds a "second-glass lockout," a smart-lock feature that physically delays access to anything the AI has already decided you don't deserve.
