## What this map covers

New Orleans' culture lives at the intersection of music and food and history, all within a walkable grid. This trail strings together:

- **5 jazz venues** — from Preservation Hall's legendary acoustic sets to the lively street-level clubs of Frenchmen Street
- **6 Creole & Cajun restaurants** — from casual Cajun dives to the oldest continuously operated restaurant in America
- **4 historic landmarks** — Jackson Square, St. Louis Cathedral, The Cabildo (where the Louisiana Purchase was signed), and the French Market

## Top stops

### Preservation Hall *(Jazz Venue)*
The most iconic jazz room in the world. Nightly sets at 8, 9, and 10 pm in an intimate, no-frills hall where the music does all the talking. Buy tickets online or line up early for general admission.

### Café du Monde *(Creole & Cajun)*
Open 24 hours since 1862, this French Market institution is the definitive New Orleans snack: three beignets buried in powdered sugar alongside chicory café au lait. Wear dark clothes.

### Antoine's Restaurant *(Creole & Cajun)*
America's oldest family-run restaurant, operating continuously since 1840. Invented Oysters Rockefeller. Its 14 individually decorated dining rooms are time capsules of New Orleans elegance.

### Frenchmen Street *(Jazz Venues)*
Just outside the French Quarter in the Marigny, this three-block strip is the soul of New Orleans' live music scene. The Spotted Cat, Snug Harbor, and Blue Nile all cluster here, with music spilling out into the street most nights.

### Jackson Square *(Historic Landmark)*
The historic heart of the city, framed by St. Louis Cathedral, the Pontalba Buildings, and views of the Mississippi River levee. Street artists and musicians set up here daily.

## How to use this map

1. **Landmarks first (daytime):** Start at Jackson Square, St. Louis Cathedral, and The Cabildo in the morning when they're least crowded. Pick up beignets at Café du Monde and walk the French Market.
2. **Lunch or early dinner:** Antoine's, Brennan's, or Galatoire's for a sit-down Creole meal; The Gumbo Shop or Coop's Place for something more casual.
3. **Evening jazz:** Walk to Preservation Hall for an 8 pm show (plan to arrive by 7:15 for admission). Then head to Frenchmen Street — The Spotted Cat, Blue Nile, and Snug Harbor all have music running through midnight and beyond.

## Verify before you go

- **Hours and admission** at historic sites (The Cabildo, St. Louis Cathedral) can change; check official sites before visiting.
- **Restaurant reservations** are strongly recommended for Antoine's, Brennan's, and Galatoire's — these are popular institutions.
- **Jazz venue schedules** vary by night; check each venue's website for the evening's lineup.
- Coop's Place is **cash only** and does not admit minors.
- All information is for planning purposes. Verify current conditions, prices, and hours before your trip.
