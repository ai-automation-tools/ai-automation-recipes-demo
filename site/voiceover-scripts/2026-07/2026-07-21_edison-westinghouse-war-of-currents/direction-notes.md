# Direction Notes — The Rival He Tried to Kill With an Execution

## Tone
Dry, matter-of-fact true-crime narrator. Let the facts carry the horror instead of over-selling it — no melodrama on the animal or execution details. Cold and clinical on the numbers, wry on the Westinghouse quote, quietly triumphant on the 1893 turn, flat and ironic on the kicker.

## Pacing
- Target run time: 126 seconds
- Reading speed: ~160 wpm; slow to ~130 wpm on the final two lines ("The myth outlived...")
- Beats / pauses: beat before "Forty-four dogs." (let the list land word by word); beat after "The current failed."; beat before "He didn't. He wasn't even there."; half-beat before the closing line.

## Emphasis cues
- **lost the biggest bet of his career** — sets up the inversion of the "genius inventor" myth
- **Forty-four dogs. Six calves. Two horses.** — deliver as a flat body count, not a punchline
- **They would have done better with an axe** — Westinghouse's actual quote; deadpan delivery, don't play it for laughs
- **He didn't. He wasn't even there.** — the myth-bust; this is the twist the whole script is building to

## B-roll / visual hooks (optional)
- Archival-style engraving/illustration of dueling power lines or a spark-gap generator, not literal animal footage
- On-screen number treatment for "700 volts" / "1,030 volts" / "1893"
- Old World's Fair nighttime photo or illustration for the Chicago beat
- Coney Island / Luna Park era postcard imagery for the kicker

## Music / SFX (optional)
Low electrical hum/crackle under the open and the Kemmler beat, cutting out sharply on "The current failed." Rebuild to a brighter, resolving tone under the 1893 win, then drop back to a hollow, quiet tone for the myth kicker.
