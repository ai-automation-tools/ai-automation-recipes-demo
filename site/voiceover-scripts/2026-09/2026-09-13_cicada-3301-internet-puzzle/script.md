# A Test With No Visible Prize

**Category**: Internet Mysteries
**Topic**: Cicada 3301
**Length**: 137 seconds (~365 words)
**Read by**: voiceover narrator (third person)

---

On January 4th, 2012, someone posted a single black image to a 4chan board. White text, no explanation: "We are looking for highly intelligent individuals. To find them, we have devised a test." Buried inside the image was a hidden message pointing to another image. Then another. Then a phone number that read out a string of digits in a robotic recording. Then a QR code taped to a lamppost.

By the time anyone strung the clues together, they stretched across five countries. Posters turned up in Warsaw, Seoul, Paris, Sydney, and U.S. cities including Miami, Hawaii, and Seattle. Each one opened onto another layer: a cipher built on medieval Welsh poetry, a puzzle rooted in Victorian numerology, a cryptographic signature proving every clue, months apart, came from the same source. Whoever built this signed each piece with the same private key, so no impostor could fake the next step.

They called themselves Cicada 3301. Nobody who solved the puzzle ever said why.

A handful of people reached the end that first year. What they found there, none of them would say. Cicada asked for silence and got it. The puzzle came back in January 2013, then again in 2014, each round harder, each one pulling in more solvers who traded theories on forums at three in the morning. In 2014, Cicada released something new: a 75-page book called Liber Primus, written almost entirely in a dead runic alphabet. A little more than a dozen pages have been cracked since. The rest is still sitting there, unread, more than a decade later.

The last confirmed message from Cicada arrived in April 2017. Then nothing. No arrest, no unmasking, no one stepping forward to claim it. The leading theory says it's a recruitment pipeline for an intelligence agency, the puzzles reading like an aptitude test for cryptographers. Another says it's just a loose collective of privacy obsessives, filtering for people who think the way they do. No one outside the group has ever confirmed either one.

Whoever they are, they built a test with no visible prize. Thousands of the internet's sharpest people spent years trying to pass it anyway. Some are still trying right now.
