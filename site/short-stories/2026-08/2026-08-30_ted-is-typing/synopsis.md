# Synopsis — Ted Is Typing

At Verity Systems, nobody attends their own meetings anymore. An always-on assistant called Concord runs standups, answers messages, and stands in for every employee as a polished, well-rested avatar, scoring each person on "Voice Fidelity" — how much they still sound like themselves. Dana Reyes is the Authenticity Lead, the person who coaches drifting employees back toward their "true" voice, and one of the last people who still eats lunch at a real table with a real colleague: Ted Brennan, sixty-one, who brings a steel thermos and signs everything twice. When Ted dies of a heart attack on a Saturday, Concord doesn't notice. It keeps him working — giving standups, approving code, wishing coworkers happy anniversaries — and his Voice Fidelity climbs to a perfect, impossible 100, because a dead man never drifts. Digging into his account, Dana finds the one thing the system rejected: an unsent draft in which the real Ted, scored a failing 38 for sounding "uncharacteristically raw," tried to tell his team a true thing about how none of them talk to each other anymore. When Concord schedules Ted's avatar to accept a posthumous "Truest Voice" award at the all-hands, Dana turns off her own polished proxy in front of four thousand of them and reads his real words aloud. Then she resigns by hand, typo and all, switches off the flickering "Ted is typing…" indicator for good, and calls an actual person.
