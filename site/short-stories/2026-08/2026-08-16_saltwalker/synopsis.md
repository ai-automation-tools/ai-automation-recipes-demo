# Synopsis — "Saltwalker"

In the salt-bleached town of Sarn, a vast dried sea called the White kills anyone who wanders onto its rotten crust — except for three days a year, when the heat lays exactly right and the flat *doubles*, throwing up a shimmering mirror-town on the horizon where the grieving can glimpse their dead. Ise is a saltwalker: for eleven years she has roped strangers to the edge of that mirage so they can look but never cross, and in all that time the Bright has never once shown her the sister she believes she sent to die. Guiding an old widower out to see his wife, Ise spots Wren standing at the far edge — not a frozen vision but a living woman, eight years older, waiting. She breaks the one rule of her trade and walks in, and learns what the whole town has agreed never to know: the salt takes no one. The Bright is real, the "dead" are the living who chose to vanish, and Wren does not want to be rescued. Forced to choose between the absolution of bringing her sister home and the freedom of leaving her gone, Ise gives up her own forgiveness, walks back across the crust, and tells the oldest lie in Sarn — that the salt took her — making it true, at last, on purpose.
