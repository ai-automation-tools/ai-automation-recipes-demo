# Synopsis

Maren Osk is the last of the Walking Cartographers, retired to a cottage at Ferry-on-Slate, missing two fingers she paid to guild vellum as a young woman when she drew ground she had never walked. In her world a sworn map binds reality: a road drawn true becomes true underfoot, but only for ground the mapmaker has walked with her own feet — the "ground truth." To draw unwalked ground is *conjecture*, a lie the vellum cannot hold, so it collects the falsehood out of the cartographer's body instead. When a soaked, desperate girl named Wren Calloway arrives, Maren refuses her outright: Wren's seventeen-year-old brother Lev lies dying of a crushed, festering leg in the Sarrow valley, sealed off after a quake dropped Harrow's Cut, the only pass, and no honest map can conjure a road over a mountain no living surveyor has walked. But Maren *has* walked to the Sarrow — forty-one years ago, over the long-vanished Ridderpass, with Jossa, who held the chain and used to say "we're on the map now." Because the binding attaches to the walking and not to the place, Maren can lay that true, dead road across the living Cassock. It will hold. It will not fold. The price is not a lie's toll but a spending: a walked road given away is a walked road you can no longer keep. She draws it anyway, and as the pen moves the memories go with the marks — the cold spring at the seventh mile, the switchback where someone laughed, the green valley, and finally the woman herself, until Maren is left weeping at a table she cannot say why, beside a second chair whose occupant she can no longer name. Across the mountains a girl climbs toward a stand of birch that has never led anywhere and now leads home. Maren has traded the only country where Jossa was still alive to save a stranger's brother — the arithmetic of her trade, paid in full.

## Cover Art

A single continuous vertical image in which a hand-inked cartographer's map — aged vellum, contour hatching, a small blue spring-mark, a dip pen and inkwell in candlelight along the bottom edge — rises seamlessly into a real, misty grey mountain, a pale road winding up its shadowed northern face to a distant, sunlit green valley at the summit. Parchment, ink-blue, cold slate and one warm patch of far green; weathered and bittersweet; no figures, no text.
