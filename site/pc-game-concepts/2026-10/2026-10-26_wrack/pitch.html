Wrack

You don't command a squad — you are a single tidal colony-creature, and every limb a hunter tears off stays on the grid as wreckage blocking your own ground until the next cycle regrows it.

Genre  Tactical RPG
Platform  PC (Windows)

Target Player

Fans of Into the Breach and Demonschool who want the next tactics game to change what a "unit" even is, not reskin the squad. They remember Shadow of Mordor's Nemesis system more fondly than the rest of that game. They're fine playing the monster — Carrion scratched that itch for an afternoon — but they want a real grid under it instead of a timer and a health bar.

Setting

A drowned coastline of tidal flats, rusted harvesting platforms, and half-submerged church spires. Your creature is translucent and bioluminescent, more coral colony than monster — it pulses along its nerve-lines instead of roaring. Drowned teal and rust at low tide; bruise-purple and cold bone-white glow at high tide.

Core Loop
30-Second Loop

Move and act each limb once — Claw, Frond, Eye-stalk, Maw, Anchor. Confirm the turn and the hunters respond. A limb at zero Integrity severs on the spot, becomes wreckage blocking its own tile, and spikes the shared Vitality pool.

30-Minute Loop

A Tide Season runs eight Cycles. Pick a feeding ground, fight a string of hunters against its tide window, bank Biomass. Back at the Shallows, regrow severed limbs, choose a Mutation for each, and decide whether to return a limb short or burn a Cycle waiting.

Key Features
  • Shared Vitality, Local Integrity — one pool underlies every independently-acting limb, so losing a limb is a systemic cost to the whole creature, not a swapped-out character.
  • Wreckage as Self-Made Terrain — a severed limb stays on the grid as cover and a chokepoint for the rest of the fight, reshaping the map with your own battle damage.
  • Mark-and-Rend Combos — an Eye-stalk's Mark sets up a Rend from any other limb, and knocking a hunter into your own wreckage adds an Impale tick.
  • Tide Windows — Low Water surfaces every limb but strips your cover; High Water hides you but only long-reach limbs can act. Timing the tide is a weapon.
  • The Flenser — a named rival hunter who's never permanently dead, remembers which limb beat them, and comes back next Cycle with gear built to counter it.
  • Regrowth as Build Choice — every severed limb regrows with a fresh Mutation pick, so your creature's loadout drifts across the Season based on what it actually lost.
Why This Stands Apart in Tactical RPG
  • You play the monster's limbs, not a squad: one shared Vitality pool underlies every independently-acting Claw, Frond, Eye-stalk, and Maw.
  • Your own wreckage becomes the map: a severed limb stays on the grid as cover and a chokepoint, so the battlefield is partly built from damage you already took.
  • A single hunter persists across the whole Season and counters your last fight, not a bigger number: the difficulty ramp is one person learning you.
  • Tide state decides who has the advantage, not turn order: Low Water and High Water are two different places to fight from, with real tradeoffs on both sides.

6-Week Prototype Path

Week 1 builds the grid, three limb types, and the sever-to-wreckage conversion. Week 2 adds the Mark-and-Rend combo and a full five-limb creature. Week 3 wires up Culling Crew AI and one complete encounter. Week 4 layers in tide windows and the Surveyor. Week 5 builds the Shallows hub, Biomass, and limb regrowth across two Cycles. Week 6 delivers a Flenser vertical slice for external playtesting, tuned against where runs actually fail.
