Minnesota Tater Tot Hotdish

Contains: dairy, gluten, fish, soy

"Hotdish" is the Minnesota and Upper Midwest word for a one-dish baked casserole: a protein, vegetables, a creamy canned-soup binder and a starch on top. Tater tot hotdish, with its lid of crisp tots, is the best-known version and a fixture at church suppers, potlucks and family reunions. Where the name comes from is not documented; one cook attributes it to Lutheran church potlucks, where a "hot dish" was set apart from cold salads and desserts. Families argue over the details, such as cheese or no cheese, one can of soup or two, and green beans or mixed vegetables.

Ingredients

Main Components

  • 1 lb (450 g) lean ground beef (90% lean works well)
  • 1 tbsp olive oil
  • 1 large yellow onion (about 1½ cups / 200 g), chopped
  • 2 ribs celery, chopped
  • 3 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1 cup (140 g) frozen green beans
  • 1 cup (140 g) frozen corn kernels
  • 2 cans (10.5 oz / 300 g each) condensed cream of mushroom soup (reduced-sodium if you like)
  • ½ cup (120 ml) milk, only if the filling looks very thick
  • 2 tbsp ketchup
  • 1 tbsp Worcestershire sauce
  • ¼ tsp black pepper
  • Salt, to taste (go light; the soup, tots and cheese are already salty)

For the Topping

  • 32 oz (900 g) frozen tater tots, straight from the freezer
  • 1½ cups (170 g) shredded cheddar cheese
  • Cooking spray, for the dish

Substitutions:

  • Green beans and corn → 2 cups frozen mixed vegetables (more old-school flavor)
  • Cheddar → omit for the plainer version many Minnesota families make
  • Ground beef → ground turkey or bison (cook to 165°F / 74°C for turkey)
  • Cream of mushroom soup → a homemade or gluten-free cream soup (changes allergens; adjust the label)

Equipment

  • 9×13-inch (23×33 cm) baking dish
  • Large skillet or Dutch oven
  • Instant-read thermometer
  • Cheese grater (optional; pre-shredded cheese works)

Instructions

Preparation
1
Heat the oven: Set the rack in the middle and preheat to 350°F (175°C). Coat the baking dish with cooking spray.
2
Prep the vegetables: Chop the onion and celery and mince the garlic. Leave the green beans and corn frozen.
Cooking
3
Brown the beef: In a large skillet or Dutch oven over medium heat, cook the ground beef, breaking it into small crumbles, for 6-8 minutes, until no pink remains. Ground beef must reach 160°F (71°C). Spoon off most of the fat, leaving about 1 tablespoon in the pan.
4
Soften the aromatics: Add the olive oil, onion and celery to the beef. Cook 4-5 minutes, stirring, until the onion is soft and translucent. Add the garlic and cook 30 seconds, until fragrant.
5
Build the filling: Stir in the green beans, corn, soup, ketchup, Worcestershire and pepper. Simmer 5 minutes, stirring, until the mixture is bubbling and thick but spoonable. Add the milk only if it is stiff. Taste and add a pinch of salt if needed.
6
Assemble: Spread the filling evenly in the dish. Arrange the frozen tots in tight rows over the top in a single layer, then scatter the cheddar over them.
7
Bake: Bake uncovered 40-45 minutes, until the tots are deep golden and crisp, the cheese is melted and the filling bubbles at the edges. The center should read at least 165°F (74°C).
Assembly & Finishing
8
Rest and serve: Let the hotdish stand 10 minutes so the filling sets and cools enough to eat. Scoop out squares with a wide spatula so the tots stay on top.

Food Safety & Storage

  • Minimum safe internal temperature: 160°F (71°C) for the ground beef when browned; 165°F (74°C) for the finished casserole
  • Storage: Refrigerate within 2 hours of cooking, in shallow covered containers. Keeps 3-4 days.
  • Freezing: Yes. Cool completely, wrap well and freeze up to 3 months. Thaw overnight in the refrigerator.
  • Reheating: Reheat to 165°F (74°C) in a 350°F (175°C) oven, covered at first, then uncovered to re-crisp the tots. Microwaving works for single portions but softens the tots.
  • Wash hands, board and utensils after handling raw beef.

Serving Suggestions

  • Serve with a simple green salad, dill pickles, or cranberry sauce on the side
  • Extra ketchup at the table is traditional
  • Dinner rolls or buttered bread for a church-supper spread
  • Milk, iced tea or a light lager

Scaling Notes

Half recipe (½×): Use an 8×8-inch (20 cm) dish and ½ lb beef. Use 1 can of soup. Bake 35-40 minutes.

Double recipe (2×): Use two 9×13-inch dishes on separate racks, swapping their positions halfway. Add 5-10 minutes if the oven is crowded. Brown the beef in batches.

Chef's Notes

  • Sourcing: Everything is a supermarket staple. Look for tots in the freezer aisle and check labels, as some contain wheat or other allergens.
  • Make-ahead: Assemble the filling in the dish up to 24 hours ahead and refrigerate, covered. Add the tots and cheese just before baking, and allow 5-10 extra minutes from a cold dish.
  • Variations: Skip the cheese for the plainer version. Use 1½-2 cans of soup for a saucier dish. Use mixed vegetables in place of green beans and corn. Bison also works well.
  • Common pitfalls: A watery filling comes from skipping the 5-minute simmer or from too much frozen veg liquid. Soggy tots come from crowding or from thawing them first. Skimping on the rest time makes it runny.
  • Rich dish note: This is a hearty, salty comfort casserole by design. Most of the sodium comes from the soup and tots. Reduced-sodium soup and light salting help, but it stays a special-occasion meal, best with a fresh, unsalted side.
  • Synthesis: This recipe blends two sources: the cheese, ketchup and Worcestershire version for its ratios, and the celery-and-garlic version for flavor depth.
