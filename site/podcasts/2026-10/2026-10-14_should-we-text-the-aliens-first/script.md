# Should We Text the Aliens First?

**Date**: 2026-10-14
**Host**: Juno Partridge
**Cast**: Dr. Odalys Venn, Barnaby "Beacon" Okafor-Lund, Professor Thessaly Crane, Gus Fennimore

## Topic Description

For decades we've mostly listened for signals from the stars. But a growing crowd of scientists, artists and backyard radio hobbyists want to go further and *transmit*: beam deliberate messages at nearby star systems and say hello first. Is that a brave first step or the cosmic equivalent of shouting your home address into a dark forest? This episode digs into four angles: the risk of being found by something that shouldn't find us, who gets to speak for eight billion people, whether any message we send could even be understood, and the awkward fact that we've been leaking TV and radar into space for a hundred years anyway.

## Script

### Juno Partridge

Good evening, night owls, insomniacs, and whoever left this stream running while they fell asleep. This is the show where we take one big strange question and let four very confident people argue about it until it's on fire. Tonight's question: should humanity send a message to the aliens, on purpose, before they've said a word to us? Not listening. *Transmitting.* Hitting "send" on the universe. Let's meet the panel. On my left, astrophysicist, risk analyst and professional party-pooper, Dr. Odalys Venn. Across from her, a man who built a radio dish on his allotment and has been, quote, "saying hello" to nearby stars for six years: Barnaby "Beacon" Okafor-Lund. Nine-metre dish, he wants you to know. The marrows get the rest of the plot. Our contrarian-in-residence, linguist and philosopher Professor Thessaly Crane, who disagrees with whoever spoke last. And finally, thirty years on cruise ships as a crooner, magician and karaoke host, a man who believes every audience can be won: Gus Fennimore. Odalys, let's go straight for the jugular. Why shouldn't we say hello?

### Dr. Odalys Venn

Because it's irreversible. You can't un-send a photon. Once a strong, deliberate, obviously artificial signal leaves Earth, it travels at light speed forever, and we lose all control over who receives it. We know nothing about who's out there. Nothing. Maybe they're lovely. Maybe they're the cosmic equivalent of a hornet's nest. There's a hypothesis, the "dark forest," that says if the galaxy is full of civilizations who can't trust each other, the smart ones stay quiet and the loud ones get... removed. I'm not saying it's true. I'm saying you don't test it with the only planet you've got.

### Barnaby "Beacon" Okafor-Lund

Ohh, here we go, the Dark Forest! Odalys, any civilization that can cross light-years has had to survive its own nuclear weapons, its own climate, its own idiots. You don't get to interstellar travel by being a hornet. You get there by learning to cooperate. Somebody's gotta say hello first, and I'd rather it was us than nobody, forever, in a silent universe full of introverts. Copy that, cosmos!

### Juno Partridge

The chat is *feral* already. User "PulsarPete" says, "Barnaby is the guy who waves at strangers on trains and somehow it works." And user "QuietPlease_88" says, "Odalys is right, the forest is dark and we are a bloke with a torch, singing."

### Gus Fennimore

Singing's not the problem, it's the *setlist*. You lead with a weak number in a dark forest, of course you get eaten. I've played the Bay of Biscay in a force-nine gale, folks. Tough room, tough galaxy.

### Juno Partridge

Hold that thought, Gus, I'm putting it in a jar. Thessaly, you've been smirking.

### Professor Thessaly Crane

I have been smirking because both of them are arguing about the consequences of a letter neither of them has written, in a language nobody shares, delivered to an address that may be empty. Odalys fears the reply. Barnaby longs for it. How adorable. I'm not convinced anyone could understand a word we said. And before Barnaby shouts "prime numbers" at me, you count because you have ten fingers and a primate's obsession with sorting fruit. A mind that evolved as, say, a chemical gradient drifting across a gas giant might not experience "one thing, then another thing" at all. You'd be sending a sonnet to a weather system.

### Dr. Odalys Venn

I actually agree with that, which is frightening. If they can't understand us, the only information they get is that we exist and roughly where we are. That's the worst possible trade: all risk, no conversation.

### Professor Thessaly Crane

Ah, careful, Odalys. Agreeing with me is how people end up wrong. If they can't understand the content, they also can't understand that we're tasty. Mutual incomprehension cuts both ways.

### Juno Partridge

Let's make this concrete. In 1974 the Arecibo radio telescope beamed a message at a star cluster about twenty-five thousand light-years away. A little picture, made of 1,679 bits. Barnaby, you've got a tattoo of it, haven't you?

### Barnaby "Beacon" Okafor-Lund

(sniffs) Left shoulder. Little stick-figure human, the DNA helix, the telescope itself. Nineteen seventy-four, Juno. People in flares sat in a control room and said, "We're here." Thessaly can call it a sonnet to the weather if she likes. I get misty every time.

### Gus Fennimore

See, now, with respect to the flares, that's a beautiful gesture but terrible staging. Little stick man? No eye contact, no smile, no hook. If I'd produced it, you open with a big brassy number, couple of jokes, quick card trick, *then* the DNA. You close on a ballad. Always leave 'em wanting more, or at least not wanting to invade.

### Dr. Odalys Venn

Gus, please don't take this personally, but the idea of you producing humanity's first impression is the single most frightening thing I've heard this year, and I read asteroid impact reports for fun.

### Gus Fennimore

(laughs) Darling, you haven't seen my closer. Ba-dum-tss.

### Juno Partridge

Which brings us neatly to angle two. Who decides? Barnaby, you're broadcasting from an allotment. Who gave you permission to speak for the species?

### Barnaby "Beacon" Okafor-Lund

Who gave anyone permission to stay *quiet* for the species? Silence is a choice too, you know. Nobody voted for that either.

### Dr. Odalys Venn

No, no. That's a false symmetry. Staying quiet is reversible. We can start talking tomorrow. We can never stop having talked. If an action can't be undone and affects every living person and every future one, the bar for consent should be enormous. Some international process, not one man standing between the courgettes and the shed.

### Juno Partridge

Chat's split down the middle. "OrbitOlive" says, "Global vote, but only people who've finished a jigsaw over a thousand pieces." Thessaly, would a vote fix this?

### Professor Thessaly Crane

A vote would be worse. Imagine the campaign. Eight billion people arguing about what to say to beings we can't describe, in a medium they may not perceive. You'd end up with a message designed by committee, and committees produce exactly one thing: beige. If we must speak, the message should be written by one strange, brave person. Odalys wants a treaty, Barnaby wants a free-for-all. I want an author.

### Juno Partridge

Gus has his hand up. Gus, put your hand down, you are not the author. Okay, the bit I've been waiting for. Odalys, here's the awkward fact. We've been leaking into space for a hundred years. Radio, television, military radar, the lot. The aliens may already have heard every game show we've ever made. Isn't the horse halfway to Alpha Centauri?

### Dr. Odalys Venn

That's the most common objection, and it's mostly wrong. Leakage is weak and scattered. Thanks to the inverse-square law, most of it fades into the background noise quite quickly. A deliberate beam, aimed at one star, with a structured, obviously artificial pattern, is a completely different thing. Leakage is mumbling in your sleep. Active messaging is standing on the roof with a megaphone, reading out your postcode.

### Barnaby "Beacon" Okafor-Lund

First of all, they're *marrows*, Odalys, not courgettes. I've been sitting on that for five minutes. Second, some of that radar is *powerful*! The big defence radars, the planetary radars bouncing off asteroids. If somebody's got a big enough ear, they've heard us already. So the forest knows. The only question is whether they hear a terrified species going quiet, or a friendly one saying, "Hello, we noticed you noticing."

### Professor Thessaly Crane

Ah, but *which* hello? That's my whole complaint. If they've heard our leakage, what they've actually received is a century of advertising jingles, weather reports and men shouting at football. That *is* our message. The real first impression has already gone out, and it's a sitcom laugh track.

### Gus Fennimore

Now *that* I resent. Some of those laugh tracks were very good. A laugh track is just a hello with a rhythm section.

### Juno Partridge

Hold on, though. Thessaly, ten minutes ago you said they couldn't understand anything we sent. Now you're worried they think we're a sitcom. Which is it?

### Professor Thessaly Crane

(pauses) Both, obviously. They cannot understand it, and yet they will judge it. That's not a contradiction, Juno. That's every first date in history.

### Juno Partridge

Putting that in the jar too. The jar is getting full. Gus, I want you in this properly. Forget the showmanship for a second. Why do you actually care?

### Gus Fennimore

(sighs) Alright. Thirty years, I've watched people walk into a ship's lounge on the first night. Strangers. Nervous. Arms folded. And there's always a moment, about the third song, when somebody at the back decides to be the first one to clap. And then the whole room changes. I'm not saying the aliens are a ship's lounge. I'm saying I've never once seen a room get warmer because everybody sat in silence waiting to find out who was dangerous.

### Professor Thessaly Crane

And there, accidentally, is the hidden assumption in Odalys's whole position. She thinks listening is neutral. It isn't. If every civilization is careful, every civilization is waiting for someone else to clap first. The galaxy becomes a library where nobody ever speaks above a whisper. Very safe. Very dead.

### Dr. Odalys Venn

It *is* a beautiful story, Gus. But in a ship's lounge, the worst case is an awkward evening. Out there, the worst case is permanent. I'm not against clapping. I'm against clapping before we've checked whether the room is on fire. And Thessaly, respectfully, no. A quiet library is still full of books. A burnt one isn't. Give me fifty more years of serious listening: bigger telescopes, better surveys. If we hear something, then at least we know who we're talking to.

### Juno Partridge

Ooh. The chat just exploded. "NebulaNan" says, "Odalys has been waiting all night to say that." Barnaby, she wants fifty years. What do you want?

### Barnaby "Beacon" Okafor-Lund

I want us to stop being scared of the sky! I was nine when I first saw Saturn through a little telescope, and I thought, "Somebody else is looking at something right now and thinking the same thing." Six years I've been sending prime numbers and a little drawing of my dog Biscuit to stars I can name. No reply, no invasion. Just the feeling that we're not hiding. If we're going to die of something, I'd rather it wasn't loneliness. And Biscuit is a very good ambassador.

### Gus Fennimore

Now *that's* an opener. The dog act. Never fails, folks. Not once, not in thirty years.

### Juno Partridge

Final round, one sentence each. Should humanity text the aliens first? Odalys.

### Dr. Odalys Venn

Not yet: listen harder, decide together, and remember you can't un-send a photon.

### Juno Partridge

Barnaby.

### Barnaby "Beacon" Okafor-Lund

Yes, today, with joy, and somebody please fund a bigger dish. Seventy-threes, everyone, over.

### Juno Partridge

Thessaly.

### Professor Thessaly Crane

Only once we have something worth saying, which, judging by tonight, is not yet.

### Juno Partridge

Gus.

### Gus Fennimore

Send the dog, then the ballad. Goodnight, galaxy, you've been a lovely audience.

### Juno Partridge

There you have it: one vote for patience, one for a dog, one for a ballad, and one for getting cleverer first. Whatever's out there, if you're listening tonight, we're sorry about the laugh tracks. Goodnight, night owls.
